Scope and assumptions
---------------------
This assessment is based on a repository scan of source code, service configuration,
infrastructure code, tests, README.md, and the existing ENTERPRISE_IMPROVEMENTS.md.
It was not produced from a running production environment or a live threat model.

The current codebase is best treated as an early-to-mid product implementation. It
has a useful microservice outline, a secured API gateway, durable patient, billing,
appointment, EHR, notification, and audit workflows, versioned Kafka integration,
OpenAPI dependencies, Dockerfiles, and prototype analytics capabilities. Phase 3
completion and its operating boundaries are recorded below.

It is not yet enterprise-grade for real patient data. The largest gaps are durable
persistence, authorization depth, compliance controls, clinical data modeling,
interoperability, operational readiness, reliable messaging, testing depth, and
real business workflows.

Regulatory scope must be confirmed by the product owner and legal/compliance team.
The project already mentions HIPAA, so this report uses HIPAA and US interoperability
references as anchors. If the product is deployed outside the US, add the relevant
local healthcare, privacy, data residency, and breach notification requirements.
This document is engineering guidance, not legal advice.


Implementation update - 2026-09-07: Phase 3
-----------------------------------------

Phase 3 is complete for the repository's workflow-API scope:

- Insurance, pharmacy, portal, and staff services now persist records in isolated
  PostgreSQL databases with Flyway migrations, constraints, indexes, and transactional
  concurrency control. H2 is test-only for those four services.
- Eligibility requires current recorded payer evidence; absent evidence stays
  PENDING. Claims validate policy/invoice ownership, preserve submitted amounts,
  enforce adjudication transitions, record remittances, and reconcile to billing.
- Billing persists invoices and immutable payment/remittance postings. Reference
  reuse is checked; invoice locks prevent concurrent overpayment; account and
  invoice balances update atomically. Billing API routing is available at the gateway.
- Pharmacy receipts persist batches and movements. Dispensing requires pharmacist
  review, a current EHR safety check, and sufficient unexpired stock. FEFO batch
  consumption, charges, movements, and completion are atomic and retry-safe.
- Portal ownership uses verified subject-to-patient bindings. Proxy grants are
  scoped, expiring, revocable, and renewable. Appointment request/cancellation and
  staff resolution, reviewed record release/download, and payment reconciliation
  persist state and actor history. Downloads recheck current access.
- Staff APIs provide role-specific queues, assignment/claim, handoff, comments,
  due dates, history, and scheduled overdue escalation. Clinical alerts can create
  idempotent urgent tasks. Prescribing blocks recorded allergy matches, duplicate
  active medications, and configured interaction pairs; closed encounters reject
  new linked records. Active prescriptions can be discontinued.
- New services emit the existing PHI-minimized HTTP audit contract. CI retains test
  reports, isolated-service logs, and a performance report.

- Registration no longer depends on a synchronous billing call. Patient state and a

- billing-service persists one account per patient and treats REST, gRPC, and patient
  event commands idempotently using database uniqueness and transactional handling.
- appointment-service persists schedules, appointments, waitlists, telehealth sessions,

- Patient, billing, appointment, EHR, and notification HTTP requests automatically emit

Major evidence from the original 2026-08-27 scan (historical baseline; superseded

What is needed for a full-fledged enterprise patient management system
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Product and domain foundation

- Define the exact product type: clinic PMS, hospital EHR/PMS, outpatient EMR,
  revenue-cycle system, patient portal, or integrated platform.
- Define user personas: patient, guardian/proxy, receptionist, nurse, clinician,
  pharmacist, lab staff, billing officer, claims officer, administrator, auditor,
  external provider, and support engineer.
- Define organization hierarchy: tenant, facility, department, ward/unit, room,
  practitioner, care team, payer, employer, pharmacy, lab, and external partner.
- Define clinical safety expectations, availability targets, regulatory markets,
  data retention periods, disaster recovery targets, and support model.

2. Patient master and registration

- Add a Master Patient Index (MPI) or patient identity service.
- Support MRN and external identifiers with issuing authority, facility, status,
  and identifier history.
- Support duplicate detection, merge/unmerge, survivorship rules, and audit trails.
- Add demographics beyond name/email/address: phone, sex/gender fields appropriate
  to jurisdiction, language, marital status, contact preference, emergency contacts,
  next of kin, guarantor, guardian/proxy, employer, and communication consent.
- Add patient status lifecycle: active, inactive, deceased, restricted, VIP/sensitive,
  merged, archived.
- Add privacy markers and consent flags that can influence authorization decisions.
- Add address normalization and validation, optional geocoding, and contact
  verification workflows.

3. Clinical EHR capabilities

- Replace record-only EHR skeletons with durable clinical resources and workflows:
  encounters, visits, complaints, vitals, allergies, conditions/problems, diagnoses,
  procedures, medications, prescriptions, immunizations, observations, care plans,
  clinical notes, documents, lab orders, lab results, imaging orders, imaging reports,
  referrals, discharge summaries, and clinical tasks.
- Add clinical coding support: ICD-10 for diagnoses, SNOMED CT where licensed,
  LOINC for observations/labs, RxNorm/NDC for medications, CPT/HCPCS where applicable,
  and local code mappings as needed.
- Add versioning, amendments, addenda, sign-off, co-sign, chart-locking, corrected
  result workflows, and non-repudiation for clinical notes.
- Add clinical decision support hooks for allergies, contraindications, duplicate
  therapy, medication interactions, abnormal lab alerts, and preventive-care reminders.
- Add patient-context safety controls: break-glass access, sensitive chart flags,
  emergency access reason capture, and post-access review.

4. Appointment and scheduling

- Model providers, rooms, equipment, appointment types, visit reasons, location,
  duration, capacity, recurring schedules, holidays, blackout periods, overbooking,
  cancellations, rescheduling, and no-shows.
- Enforce conflict detection transactionally. The current in-memory schedule cannot
  prevent double booking across instances.
- Add waitlist promotion, reminder timing, check-in/check-out, arrival status,
  encounter creation, telehealth session lifecycle, consent capture, and cancellation
  rules.
- Integrate scheduling with patient, provider, notification, billing, and portal
  workflows.

5. Billing, payments, insurance, and claims

- Replace the hard-coded billing gRPC response with persisted billing accounts,
  invoices, invoice line items, charge capture, payments, adjustments, refunds,
  write-offs, account balance, statements, and reconciliation.
- Add service catalog and pricing rules, facility/provider-specific price books,
  tax/discount rules where applicable, and authorization requirements.
- Implement insurance eligibility verification, coverage rules, claim submission,
  claim adjudication, denials, appeals, remittance posting, coordination of benefits,
  patient responsibility calculation, and payment plans.
- For US payer integrations, plan for X12 270/271 eligibility, 837 claims, 835 ERA,
  276/277 claim status, and prior authorization workflows. Whether these are direct
  integrations or clearinghouse integrations is a product decision.
- Add payment gateway integration with tokenized cards/mobile money/bank transfers
  depending on market, PCI scope reduction, receipts, refunds, and audit.

6. Pharmacy and inventory

- Persist supplies, medication stock, batches/lots, expiry dates, controlled-substance
  handling where applicable, stock movements, receiving, transfers, adjustments,
  reorder thresholds, purchase orders, suppliers, dispensing, returns, wastage,
  and inventory valuation.
- Link prescriptions to pharmacy dispensing and billing charges.
- Add medication safety checks, substitutions, refill handling, partial dispense,
  and patient instructions.

7. Notifications and communications

- Add delivery providers for email, SMS, push, and optionally WhatsApp or voice,
  depending on market.
- Add templates, localization, patient preferences, opt-in/opt-out, quiet hours,
  delivery retries, bounce/failure handling, rate limiting, and delivery status.
- Drive notifications mostly through events rather than direct manual POST calls.
- Avoid putting PHI in messages unless allowed by consent and policy.

8. Audit, compliance, and privacy operations

- Make audit event capture automatic through HTTP filters, gRPC interceptors,
  service methods, database write hooks, and event consumers.
- Store audit events in an append-only durable store with tamper evidence, retention,
  indexing, and export support.
- Include actor id, actor role, patient id, resource type/id, action, outcome,
  reason, source service, endpoint, request id, correlation id, IP/device where
  applicable, timestamp, and before/after references without leaking full PHI.
- Add audit queries for patient access reports, staff access reports, break-glass
  review, failed access attempts, administrative changes, data exports, and incident
  investigations.
- Add formal support for access reviews, privilege recertification, sanctions process,
  breach investigation, and evidence export.

9. Interoperability

- Establish a healthcare interoperability strategy rather than inventing all payloads.
- Add FHIR APIs for core administrative and clinical resources. At minimum map the
  internal model to Patient, Practitioner, Organization, Encounter, Appointment,
  Observation, DiagnosticReport, MedicationRequest, MedicationDispense, Condition,
  AllergyIntolerance, Immunization, DocumentReference, Claim, ExplanationOfBenefit,
  and AuditEvent where applicable.
- Choose a FHIR version/profile strategy. FHIR R4 is widely used and stable; R4B/R5
  may be relevant depending on target integrations.
- Add CapabilityStatement, SMART on FHIR/OAuth scopes if exposing patient/provider
  APIs, search parameters, pagination, bundles, provenance, and conformance tests.
- Add HL7 v2 ingestion for labs/admissions if integrating with existing hospital
  systems, and DICOM/document storage if imaging is in scope.
- Add terminology service or reference data service for code systems and value sets.
- Add import/export jobs, partner credentials, consent checks, and reconciliation.

10. Enterprise security architecture

- Replace custom local JWT auth with an OAuth2/OIDC identity provider or a hardened
  internal IAM service. Candidates include Keycloak, AWS Cognito, Auth0, Azure AD
  B2C/Entra External ID, or an enterprise IdP selected by deployment context.
- Use short-lived access tokens, refresh token rotation, revocation, MFA, account
  lockout, password reset, device/session management, and login audit trails.
- Enforce authorization in every service, not only at the gateway. Use method-level
  policies and patient-level checks.
- Implement RBAC plus ABAC. Examples: role, facility, care team relationship,
  appointment relationship, patient consent, emergency access, payer relationship,
  and support impersonation approval.
- Propagate verified identity and correlation context from gateway to services with
  signed headers or token forwarding. Do not trust arbitrary incoming identity headers.
- Use TLS everywhere and mTLS for service-to-service traffic, especially gRPC.
- Move secrets to a secret manager. Do not store JWT secrets, datasource passwords,
  API keys, or local datasource credentials in source control or IDE metadata.
- Add encryption at rest using managed keys, database backups encryption, object
  storage encryption, and key rotation.
- Add rate limiting, request size limits, CORS policy, WAF where applicable, bot
  protection for patient portal, and admin action confirmation.
- Add SAST, dependency scanning, container scanning, secret scanning, SBOM generation,
  image signing, and patch management.

11. Data architecture

- Use a database-per-service pattern with explicit ownership. Start with PostgreSQL
  for transactional domains unless there is a clear reason to introduce another store.
- Add Flyway or Liquibase migrations for every service that owns a database.
- Remove hibernate ddl-auto=update from non-local environments.
- Add optimistic locking/version fields, created_at, updated_at, created_by,
  updated_by, soft delete/deactivation where clinically appropriate, and audit
  correlation fields.
- Add idempotency keys for commands that can be retried: patient creation, appointment
  booking, payments, claim submission, notification send, and external imports.
- Add referential validation through owned APIs or local read models. Do not create
  tight shared database coupling between services.
- Add backup, point-in-time recovery, restore testing, retention policies, legal
  hold, archival, and deletion/anonymization policies where legally permitted.
- For analytics, create a de-identified or pseudonymized data pipeline. Do not stream
  unnecessary direct identifiers like patient email to analytics by default.

12. Messaging and consistency

- Use a transactional outbox in services that both modify local state and publish
  events.
- Use inbox/idempotent consumer tables in services that process events.
- Add event versioning, schema compatibility rules, event timestamp, producer,
  correlation id, causation id, tenant/facility id, actor id, and event id.
- Add retry topics, dead-letter topics, poison-message handling, monitoring, and
  replay strategy.
- Add sagas for multi-service business transactions:
  - patient registration -> billing account -> notification -> audit
  - appointment booking -> notification -> encounter preparation
  - prescription -> pharmacy dispense -> billing charge -> notification
  - insurance verification -> claim submission -> remittance posting
- Define which operations require strong consistency and which can be eventual.

13. API design and developer experience

- Version APIs consistently, for example /api/v1/patients.
- Use consistent error responses, preferably application/problem+json style.
- Add pagination, sorting, filtering, field selection, ETags, idempotency keys,
  request ids, and OpenAPI examples.
- Add consistent DTO naming and validation. Prefer typed dates over string dates
  when practical.
- Add DTOs that do not expose unnecessary PHI.
- Add service-level OpenAPI generation and gateway aggregation that works in every
  environment.
- Add contract tests for REST, gRPC, and event schemas.
- Add a root Maven parent/reactor to centralize Java version, Spring Boot version,
  plugin versions, dependency management, build rules, test conventions, and shared
  quality gates.

14. Observability and operations

- Add Spring Boot Actuator to every service with health, readiness, liveness, metrics,
  and info endpoints.
- Add structured JSON logs with no PHI by default.
- Add OpenTelemetry tracing across gateway, REST calls, gRPC calls, Kafka producers,
  Kafka consumers, and database calls.
- Add Micrometer metrics with Prometheus/Grafana or a cloud-native equivalent.
- Add dashboards for service health, latency, error rate, saturation, Kafka lag,
  database health, failed logins, authorization denies, notification delivery,
  claim failures, appointment no-shows, and audit ingestion failures.
- Define SLOs and alerts. For example: auth availability, patient chart retrieval
  latency, appointment booking success, event processing lag, and backup freshness.
- Add runbooks for incidents, deployment rollback, failed migrations, message replay,
  breach investigation, and database restore.

15. Infrastructure and deployment

- Decide target runtime: Docker Compose for local development, ECS/Fargate for AWS,
  Kubernetes, or another platform. The current CDK has AWS direction but is not
  production-ready.
- Add a root local development environment that starts PostgreSQL, Kafka, services,
  and gateway with consistent environment variables.
- Remove host.docker.internal from production profiles. Use service discovery,
  internal load balancers, DNS, or service mesh.
- Use private networking for internal services and expose only the gateway and public
  frontend endpoints.
- Add TLS termination, private certificate management, service mTLS, and secure
  egress rules.
- Add separate dev, test, staging, and production configurations.
- Add autoscaling, task/container resource sizing, rolling/canary deployments,
  blue-green where appropriate, and database migration rollout strategy.
- Increase log retention beyond one day based on compliance and operations needs.
- Add backup infrastructure, object storage for documents, KMS keys, secrets manager,
  queue/topic provisioning, alarms, dashboards, and IAM least privilege.
- Fix LocalStack deployment or split "local emulation" from "real AWS deployment."

16. Testing and quality

- Add unit tests for service logic, mappers, validators, and security policies.
- Add controller tests with MockMvc/WebTestClient.
- Add repository tests with Testcontainers PostgreSQL.
- Add Kafka integration tests with Testcontainers Kafka or Redpanda.
- Add gRPC contract and integration tests.
- Add consumer-driven contract tests for service APIs and event schemas.
- Add end-to-end tests for registration, login, patient CRUD, appointment booking,
  EHR entry, billing charge, notification, audit event, and portal workflows.
- Add negative tests for authorization, data isolation, invalid tokens, expired
  tokens, patient-level access denial, duplicate records, invalid dates, and retries.
- Add migration tests and rollback/forward-only migration validation.
- Add performance/load tests for gateway, auth, patient search, appointment booking,
  and event processing.
- Add security tests: dependency scan, secret scan, SAST, container scan, dynamic API
  testing, OWASP API Top 10 checks, and penetration-test readiness.
- Use synthetic patient data only. Do not put real PHI in tests, logs, seed data,
  screenshots, or fixtures.

auth-service

Current:
- Login validates BCrypt credentials against a Flyway-managed user store.
- Short-lived JWTs contain stable subject, issuer, audience, token id, timestamps,
  email, roles, and scopes; protected endpoints enforce resource-server policies.
- Production infrastructure models the signing key and database credentials as
  managed secrets; synthetic development seed data is migration-profile isolated.

Needed:
- Restrict endpoints to only expected public routes: login, token refresh, password
  reset, health, and docs in local mode.
- Add registration/admin user management or integrate with an external IdP.
- Add MFA, password reset, account lockout, refresh token rotation, token revocation,
  session/device management, audit logs, and user lifecycle.
- Add tenant/facility and session/assurance context to the authorization model.
- Complete asymmetric signing/JWKS and signing-key rotation through the selected IdP.
- Add service-to-service authentication.

patient-service

Current:
- Persists MPI-oriented patient records with MRNs, external identifiers, demographics,
  contact details, lifecycle status, optimistic versioning, and archival.
- Supports idempotent registration, duplicate candidate search, merge/unmerge history,
  and lifecycle changes through secured APIs.
- Commits state and minimal-PHI patient.events.v1 messages to a transactional outbox;
  asynchronous consumers create the billing account and populate audit/analytics.

Needed:
- Add configurable probabilistic matching, identifier history/issuing authority, and
  formal survivorship rules beyond the current deterministic duplicate candidates.
- Add facility/tenant ownership, emergency contacts, guardians/proxies, privacy markers,
  consent flags, and verified contact workflows.
- Add patient-level authorization checks.
- Add an operator workflow for outbox poison messages and formal replay procedures.

billing-service

Current:
- Persists uniquely patient-keyed billing accounts with status, currency, balance,
  optimistic versioning, timestamps, and idempotency keys.
- Provides secured REST and gRPC account creation/read paths and consumes
  PATIENT_REGISTERED from patient.events.v1 idempotently.
- Maps validation/internal failures to proper gRPC status responses.

Needed:
- Add invoice model, line items, payments, charge capture, adjustments, refunds,
  statements, reconciliation, and ledger-like accounting behavior.
- Add billing lifecycle events through a service-owned outbox as revenue workflows
  are introduced.
- Add claim/insurance integration contracts.
- Complete gRPC TLS/mTLS, deadlines, retry policy, and contract tests.

appointment-service

Current:
- Persists schedules, appointments, waitlists, virtual consultations, consent forms,
  and minute-granularity appointment reservations.
- Enforces doctor/time uniqueness transactionally so overlapping bookings fail even
  across instances; cancellations release reserved slots.

Needed:
- Add appointment types, locations, rooms, equipment, provider availability, recurring
  schedules, no-show, check-in, check-out, rescheduling, cancellations, waitlist
  promotion, and reminder events.
- Integrate with patient-service, notification-service, staff-dashboard, billing,
  and EHR encounter creation.

ehr-service

Current:
- Persists encounters, histories, diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results, clinical
  notes, imported external lab results, and vaccinations.
- Links clinical entries to encounters, supports encounter closure, and signs/locks
  clinical notes with optimistic versioning.

Needed:
- Treat this as the highest-risk service after auth and patient. Add patient-context
  authorization, clinical terminology, detailed provenance, amendments/addenda,
  co-signature, corrected-result, retention, and non-repudiation workflows.
- Add observations/vitals, allergies, problems, medications, orders, care plans,
  clinical notes, documents, imaging, referrals, and discharge summaries.
- Add FHIR mappings and conformance tests.

insurance-service

Current:
- Has in-memory policies, coverage verifications, claims, and adjudication status.

Needed:
- Add durable payer, plan, policy, member, eligibility, prior authorization, claim,
  denial, appeal, remittance, and coordination-of-benefits models.
- Integrate with billing and external payers/clearinghouses.
- Add secure external partner credentials and audit.

inventory-pharmacy-service

Current:
- Has in-memory supplies, medications, prescriptions, dispense action, and medication
  charges.

Needed:
- Add durable inventory and pharmacy models with stock movements, batches, expiry,
  supplier/purchase order flows, controlled-drug controls where applicable, dispense
  workflows, medication safety checks, and billing integration.

notification-service

Current:
- Persists a notification delivery queue and exposes secured HTTP submission/status
  endpoints for generic notifications, appointment reminders, bill alerts, and MFA.
- Consumes notification.requests.v1, invokes a configurable webhook provider, records
  provider results, retries with backoff, and dead-letters exhausted deliveries.

Needed:
- Add channel-specific production adapters, template rendering, delivery callbacks,
  preferences, quiet hours, opt-out, localization, rate limiting, and consent-aware
  PHI-safe message policies.

audit-compliance-service

Current:
- Persists append-only, idempotent audit events with previous/event hashes and indexed
  actor, patient, resource, request, correlation, outcome, and time metadata.
- Automatically consumes PHI-minimized HTTP access events from the Phase 2 core
  services and versioned domain events; manual submission is no longer the main path.

Needed:
- Extend capture to authentication, gateway/admin, gRPC, and remaining services; add
  database-enforced immutability, key-anchored tamper evidence, retention controls,
  privileged access review, breach investigation, export, and alerting.

analytics-service

Current:
- Consumes the minimal-PHI patient.events.v1 JSON envelope and logs operational event
  identifiers rather than patient names or contact information.
- Has in-memory population health trend/cohort/report endpoints.

Needed:
- Add de-identified data ingestion, metrics warehouse/lake, reporting jobs, cohort
  rules, quality measures, regulatory report generation, dashboard APIs, and data
  governance.
- Remove direct identifiers from analytics events unless explicitly justified.

patient-portal-service

Current:
- Has in-memory appointment request, record request, payment, and overview objects.

Needed:
- Add real patient-facing workflows: authenticated portal access, profile updates,
  proxy/guardian access, appointment self-service, medical record download/export,
  secure messaging, billing/payment, consent forms, notification preferences, MFA,
  and accessibility.
- Enforce that patients can only access their own permitted data unless proxy access
  is granted.

staff-dashboard-service

Current:
- Has in-memory daily dashboard, rounds, chart summaries, and tasks.

Needed:
- Add role-specific queues for reception, nurses, clinicians, billing, pharmacy,
  labs, and administrators.
- Integrate with EHR, appointments, patient, audit, and notifications.
- Add task assignment, escalation, handoff, comments, due dates, status history,
  and authorization.

infrastructure

Current:
- AWS CDK style stack with VPC, ECS, RDS, MSK, CloudWatch Logs, and ALB concepts.
- LocalStack mode disables RDS/MSK.
- Fargate service discovery fails under current LocalStack license.
- Secrets and env vars are not production-safe.

Needed:
- Split local development orchestration from production IaC.
- Add Docker Compose for local work.
- Add deployable AWS IaC with secrets manager, IAM least privilege, private networking,
  service discovery/load balancing, RDS per owning service, MSK or managed Kafka,
  backups, log retention, metrics, alarms, TLS, WAF, and deployment pipelines.
- Stop tracking cdk.out and target outputs.

integration-tests

Current:
- Auth and patient integration tests assume services are already running at
  http://localhost:4004.

Needed:
- Add automated environment startup or Testcontainers-based integration testing.
- Expand tests to every service and key workflow.
- Add security, authorization, and negative path tests.


Recommended additional services/modules
---------------------------------------
Do not create every service immediately. These can start as modules or bounded
contexts and become services only when scale, ownership, or deployment needs justify
the split.

- Master Patient Index / identity matching service
- Organization, facility, department, room, and resource service
- Practitioner/staff credentialing and provider directory service
- Encounter/visit service if not owned inside EHR
- Orders/results service for lab, radiology, and procedures
- Document management service for clinical documents, consent forms, and attachments
- Consent and privacy preference service
- Terminology/reference data service
- Integration engine for FHIR, HL7 v2, X12, webhooks, partner APIs, and file drops
- Search service for patient and clinical search with strict access control
- Reporting/data warehouse service
- Clinical decision support service
- Admin/configuration service
- Payment gateway adapter service
- Notification preference and template management module
- Support/admin impersonation module with approval and audit

Bottom line
-----------
The repository now has the Phase 0 repository/local-development baseline, the Phase 1
secure-production foundation, and the Phase 2 durable core workflows recorded above.
Patient registration, billing-account creation, scheduling, core EHR records,
notification delivery, audit capture, and patient-domain messaging no longer rely on
the original in-memory or fire-and-forget prototypes.

Update 2026-09-07: Phase 3 workflow APIs are complete within the boundaries above.
The next implementation target is Phase 4. The system is still not approved for real patient data until the
remaining deployment release gates, compliance review, external-provider acceptance,
patient-context authorization, and later phases are completed.
